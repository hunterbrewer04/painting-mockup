The studio

We treat paint the way restorers treat gilding — as the last, most visible act of architecture.

We work in small crews, one project at a time. A single foreman stays with a house from the first survey to the final coat, so nothing is handed off and nothing is hurried.

Surfaces are diagnosed before they are dressed. We read the substrate, the moisture, the old sheens beneath, then let the cure times set the schedule rather than the other way around.

  • Preparation

    Nine-tenths of the hours are spent before the first coat — filling, sanding, sealing, until the wall is quieter than the paint that will cover it.

  • Pigment

    Full-bodied mineral paints, mixed to the light of the room. We sample each colour on the actual surface and watch it through a full day before we commit.

  • Patience

    Schedules bend to cure times, not the reverse. A finish left to settle properly outlasts one rushed to a deadline by a decade or more.
